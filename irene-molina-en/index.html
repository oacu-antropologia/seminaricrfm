Racial Capitalism: In the Shadow of the Swedish Model

14:00 · Seminar Room, Faculty of Geography and History · University of Barcelona

Abstract

The seminar explores racial capitalism in Sweden and Scandinavia, contributing to a field primarily shaped by scholarship on the United States and, to a lesser extent, the United Kingdom. Building on Cedric Robinson's concept of racial capitalism, I examine how capitalism, colonialism and racial formation developed within a Nordic context. The talk highlights three interconnected processes: Sweden's participation in settler colonialism and overseas colonial ventures; the super-exploitation of racialized populations, from Sámi, Roma and enslaved Africans to successive generations of migrant workers; and the institutionalization of racist knowledge through racial biology and eugenics. In this way, it challenges narratives of Nordic exceptionalism and reveals the enduring role of race in shaping labour, welfare, citizenship and belonging. In the seminar, Irene Molina will focus on the relation between current welfare politics, racial housing segregation and racial capitalism.

Biography

Irene Molina is a Professor in Human Geography at the Institute for Housing and Urban Research (IBF), Uppsala University. Her research revolves around social justice, racial capitalism, post-colonial feminism and urban segregation. Together with Prof. Miguel Martinez, she coordinates the Housing and Urban Justice Project (HUJP) at IBF. Apart from the recently published book Racial Capitalism. In the Shadow of the Swedish Model (co-authored with Diana Mulinari and Anders Neergaard), she has co-authored the article “Capturing Displacement: A Dialectical Mixed-Methods Approach to the Study of Renoviction”, IJURR, 2025.
